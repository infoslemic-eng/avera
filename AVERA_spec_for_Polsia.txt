====================================================================
1. WHAT AVERA IS
====================================================================
AVERA is a single-page web application for analysts of an accounting-services group (Unija ETL / ETL Global network) who buy accounting, payroll and tax-advisory firms. It replaces a complex Excel valuation model. The analyst drops the target's documents (Excel statements, CSV, PDF/DOCX term sheets, LOIs, e-mails, the company website) into the app; the app reads them, builds the P&L on a standard chart of accounts, normalises EBITDA, computes the price the deal can repay out of its own cash flow, models the acquisition loan (ETL intercompany loan rules), earn-out, put/call option, exit, working-capital price adjustment, four scenarios, taxes by jurisdiction — and renders a 5-page Investment Board paper with a signature page. Exports: JSON (save deal), Excel, PowerPoint, PDF.

Principles the app states everywhere:
- Nothing is applied on its own: every import and every term read out of a document is shown for confirmation first.
- A figure a document does not state is never invented — the field stays empty, never 0.
- Every figure in the report must have a source (register of sources).
- Files are parsed in the browser and never sent anywhere.
- Bilingual UI: Slovenian (default) and English, switch SL/EN in the header. Brand text (AVERA, the tagline, "Powered by Unija ETL") is always English.
- Projection after the last planned year is flat (same level as the last plan year) unless growth is solved/entered.
- Where a revenue multiple is shown, the app also says how many months of revenue it equals (annual revenue / 12).

5. CALCULATION RULES (shown in-app under "Methodology")
====================================================================
- Normalisations are not counted twice: adjustments already inside the "UNIJA (P&L input)" columns are flagged, shown in the booked → Unija bridge, and not deducted again.
- Revenue and costs grow together at the same rate, so the margin stays constant (the old workbook grew only EBITDA).
- Interest rate defaults to the current market rate (6.26 %); it is an input.
- Tax on a loss: no negative tax by default (switch to match the workbook).
- Every deal carries the bullet mechanics: even when the loan clears within the term, the app runs the cash-flow variant, the bullet amount and the extra months needed.
- Preferred dividend is an amount, not a percentage (60 % stake, profit 100 → buyer 60, seller 40; profit 80 → buyer still 60, seller 20); runs for the years entered.
- CapEx reduces cash before the ownership split.
- Repayment as the ETL intercompany agreement writes it: twice a year on 31 May and 30 November, interest on actual days / 360 from the first payment date after drawdown (also through the moratorium), principal in equal instalments, 12 months moratorium on principal from closing. Monthly amortisation kept only for comparison.
- Corporate tax from the jurisdiction, year by year: Croatia 10 % below EUR 1,000,000 revenue else 18 %; Slovenia 22 % (held for every year, conservative); Serbia 15 %; North Macedonia off revenue; Montenegro off profit; BiH by entity.
- Dividend withholding falls with the holding period (Parent–Subsidiary Directive / treaties): e.g. Croatia 24 months (or at once with a bank guarantee, otherwise 5 % for two years), Serbia 12 months, none in Slovenia and Bulgaria. Profit of a year is paid 30 June of the following year, so the rate is read at that date.
- Buying less than 100 %: revenue multiple is read on the 100 % value; EBITDA multiple is entered on a 100 % basis; seller's share of each year's profit shown throughout.
- The option over the rest is a second purchase: costs money in the exercise year, raises the buyer's share from then on, funded by a new loan or liquidity.
- Exit: two returns — deal IRR and equity IRR; the app never shows a zero equity IRR when there is no equity.
- Required growth instead of DSCR: the app answers "what annual growth (in % and €) would be needed for the bullet to disappear".
- Baseline scenario after the last plan year: growth solved so the deal exactly finances itself (surpluses of earlier years accumulate and cover later shortfalls).
- Self-financing price = price the cash flow repays within the term; optimal price net of costs = that minus transaction costs (financed by the same loan).
